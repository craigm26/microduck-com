Privacy

What Microduck Studio does and does not do with your data.

Last updated 14 November 2026 · applies to Microduck Studio for iPhone, iPad and Mac

There are no accounts of ours, no analytics, and no tracking. The app collects nothing about you, sends nothing about you anywhere, and contains no third-party analytics or advertising SDK of any kind. Nobody who makes this app can see that you are using it, or what you do in it.

What stays on your device

Everything you make. Policies you import, motions you write or record, plans, scenes, runs you record for Dream-1 (the duck's camera pictures and the commands you sent), and any language model you download all live in the app's own storage on your device and are never uploaded anywhere by the app.

Credentials such as a Hugging Face token, a bench token or an API key for a model service you configured are stored in the iOS Keychain. They are sent only to the service they belong to, only when you ask for something that needs them.

An Anthropic or OpenAI key for a hosted planner, and a robot tool server's token, each have their own place in the Keychain, kept on this device only and never in a backup. A key is checked for shape before it is saved, so an Anthropic key cannot be sent to OpenAI or the other way round. Forgetting a key removes it from this device but does not cancel it; you cancel it with the company that issued it.

When the app talks to the network

Only in response to something you do. The app has no background activity, no periodic sync, and no phone-home.

Dream Labs (api.dreamlabs.ai, platform.dreamlabs.ai) You save a Dream Labs key, or run Dream-1 on the duck. The key you pasted, which you keep in one of two places. Saved in Settings, it is kept in this device's Keychain and sent only to Dream Labs. Saved on the bridge screen, it goes to the robot's computer, which checks it with Dream Labs and keeps it there, and the app does not store it. While Dream-1 runs, whichever of the two holds the key sends Dream Labs the duck's last few camera pictures, its last walk command and the task you typed, about every two seconds, and stops when the run stops. From build 89 the app blurs any face it finds in those pictures, and in runs you record for Dream-1, before they leave the device or are saved; before build 89 they were sent as the camera took them. Finding faces can miss one, so keep people out of view while Dream-1 runs or records. Your key is billed by Dream Labs, and what you send is under their privacy policy, not ours.

api.anthropic.com A hosted planner you set up with your own Anthropic key chooses the duck's next step. Not offered yet; see below the table. Your key, and for each step: the goal you typed, and what you said if it was different; the duck's position, heading, whether it is standing, its last move and how far it is from the edge of its area; its last five steps in this run; the names of the moves and sounds it may choose from; and any notes from a robot tool server you added. One picture from the duck's camera goes only if you allowed the camera for planners. That is off by default, there is one switch for each company, and turning it on needs the adult's agreement first. Any face the app finds in that picture is blurred before it leaves the device, and if the app cannot look for faces, no picture goes. Anthropic bills your key for each step, and what you send is under Anthropic's privacy policy, not ours. Anthropic says it deletes API inputs and outputs within 30 days, keeps content flagged under its Usage Policy for longer, and does not train its models on what API customers send (retention, commercial terms). Microduck Studio is not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic.
api.openai.com A hosted planner you set up with your own OpenAI key chooses the duck's next step. Not offered yet; see below the table. The same as for Anthropic: your key, the goal, the duck's state, its last five steps, the names it may choose from, any tool server notes, and a camera picture only if you allowed one. Every request sets store to false, so OpenAI is asked not to keep the response for later use. OpenAI bills your key for each step, and what you send is under OpenAI's privacy policy, not ours. OpenAI says it keeps abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days, keeps any picture its checks flag as child sexual abuse material for review, and does not train its models on API data unless you opt in (your data). Microduck Studio is not affiliated with or endorsed by OpenAI.
A planner server on your network A model on a computer you set up, such as Ollama, LM Studio or llama.cpp's server, chooses the duck's next step. Not offered yet; see below the table. The same words a hosted planner gets: the goal, the duck's state, its last steps, the names it may choose from and any tool server notes, plus the access token if you typed one. Never a camera picture. The app may also ask an Ollama server which models it has, to record exactly which one answered. This is a machine at an address you typed. Over plain http, anyone on the same Wi-Fi can read what is sent, the token too; on a tailnet, Tailscale encrypts it between your devices. The app refuses plain http to an address on the public internet.
A robot tool server (MCP) you added You tap Look at its tools. A greeting that names this app and its version, requests for the server's list of tools, and the token you typed, if any. The app sends a token over plain http only to an address on your own network, and the screen says when it travels unencrypted. Your keys, Stop, the sticks and the duck's limits never go to a tool server, and the app never moves the duck through one. Nothing the server sends is kept: no cookies, no cache, and its list is shown, not saved. This is a machine at an address you typed.

Planners and robot tool servers are new in the build after build 95. In that build, when you type something for the duck that the app cannot read by itself (Tell), it can ask Apple's on-device model to choose the next step. That model runs on this device, needs no key, and nothing it reads or decides leaves the device. You can also save an Anthropic or OpenAI key, and add a robot tool server and look at its tools. Hosted planners, planners on your network and downloaded models are not offered yet: each shows only after it has been measured on the practice duck. So that build does not contact Anthropic, OpenAI or a planner server on your network, and reads no tool. The rows above say what will go once one is offered. When a planner does read a robot tool server, it reads only tools the server marks read-only that need no input, and what they say is given to the planner as information, never as the goal.

If you use an on-device model instead, nothing you type leaves the device at all, because the weights were downloaded once and everything after that is local. That includes a planner that runs on a model downloaded to this device: nothing it reads or decides leaves the device.

Files you export or share stay on your device until you send them somewhere yourself. The share sheet hands the file to whatever you pick, and the app is not told what you picked.

Permissions the app asks for

Camera

Used only in the Lab, and only when you choose to put a duck on your own floor. It finds the floor so the duck can be drawn at its real size on it. No image is recorded, stored, or transmitted. Every Lab mode also works on a rendered stage with the camera off.

Local network

Used to find computers on your own network running the physics bench, the plan router or a robot's bridge, which announce themselves over Bonjour, and to reach the ones you saved or typed in when you ask them to run something. A robot tool server or a planner server is reached only at the address you typed. The app looks only for those services, only on your own network; nothing is added until you tap Add, and nothing found is sent anywhere.

Bluetooth

Used to find a Microduck robot near you, pair with it, and ask it which software it is running. It looks for one specific service identifier belonging to the robot and ignores everything else. It is not used for location, for proximity to anything, or for any other purpose.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, children under 13 included. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and nothing it does reaches the people who make it. There is nothing to delete because nothing is gathered. On that basis Microduck Studio complies with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

Hosted planners send words, and a camera picture if you allow one, to Anthropic or OpenAI, as the table says. A hosted planner works only with a key an adult pastes in and pays for, so it receives what that adult's runs send, under that company's terms. The key screen says: "For children: a parent or guardian holds the key and agrees to the child using it." We recommend that an adult sets up any planner, holds its key and watches the duck while it runs, and that people, especially children, stay out of the camera's view.

A model, not a person

When a model chooses the duck's next step, a model chose it, not a person. The screen where you save a hosted planner key says so for each company, in these words: "Decisions come from an AI model (Anthropic, Claude Haiku 5.5), not a person." and "Decisions come from an AI model (OpenAI, GPT-6 Luna), not a person." It also says "The AI can be wrong. Check what it suggests." During a run, each step a model chose is marked on the card that asks you to go ahead and in the list at the end of the run: "Chosen by an AI model, not a person", with the provider and the model. A step the app read from your words by itself has no such mark. Settings shows the same line for a model on your network or on this device, for example "Decisions come from an AI model (Apple's on-device model, on this phone), not a person."

What we would have to tell you, and cannot

There is no data retention schedule, because nothing is retained. There is no list of third parties data is shared with, because there is no data and no sharing. There is no way to request a copy of your information from us, because we do not have any. If that changes, this page changes first, and the app's release notes will say so.
