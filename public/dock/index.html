Build a charging dock

The MicroDock v3, the egg dock: Microduck sits down, scoots back under a floating hood, rests while it charges, then scoots out and walks off. It is fully printed, with no screws and no glue, and goes together in thirteen steps.

Whose dock this is. The MicroDock v3 is designed and published by Vottivott in microduck-playground, not by Microduck Studio or Pollen Robotics. Every picture and file on this page loads straight from that repository, pinned to one commit, so what you see here is exactly what was published there. The printable parts are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: share and remix them, credit the author, not for sale.

What you need

Print

  • 1 base
  • 1 front plate
  • 1 dome
  • 1 spring stage
  • 1 hood
  • 1 tag, in white and black
  • 2 filament pins: pieces of 1.75 mm PLA filament, about 4 cm each

Get

  • A magnetic USB-C adapter pair (24-pin, passes CC so chargers switch on). The adapter goes in the hood, the tip in Microduck.
  • A 90° USB-C adapter, "horizontal": it turns the cable sideways in the hood.
  • A USB-C cable and a charger. The hood's clip holds the cable's plug.
  • Flush cutters, for the pins.
  • Removable mounting tape, for the floor.

Check your cable's plug first. The hood is shaped around that angle adapter and that plug. Print page 2 of the PDF at 100 % and hold your plug to its outline: it should match 12.3 × 20 mm, 6.4 mm thick. Plate 7 (optional) prints just the hood's middle, so you can try your adapters and the duck's tip before printing the whole hood.

Print the parts

PLA, white, plus black for the tag, on a 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.20 mm Standard. For a Bambu Lab P1S, the ready Bambu Studio project sets everything up. Plates 1 to 6 take about 9 h 15 min and 365 g of PLA.

Build it, step by step

Thirteen steps and one check, in the manual's own words. Each picture is a page of the manual; open it for the full size.

Steps 1 and 2: the spring stage onto the front plate

  1. Lay the front plate face down. Put the spring stage on it: the post's two holes onto the plate's two pegs.
  2. Push a pin through the ear, the post and the far ear, from the middle of the egg. Cut it off flush on both sides. The pin holds the post to the front plate: the push of every docking goes into the plate.

Steps 3 to 5: build the hood and cable first

  1. Plug the cable into the angle adapter's side, then the angle adapter into the magnetic adapter's back.
  2. Turn the hood upside down. Lower the three in: the magnetic face toward the hood's flared end, the cable out through the opening in the side wall.
  3. Hold it toward the flared end and press it down until it clicks. Two clips hold it: one under the angle adapter, one under the cable's plug. To take it out, push them toward the flared end and pull the three out.

Steps 6 and 7: the hood onto the stage

  1. Turn the hood back over. Stand the front plate up and push the hood's arm through its window into the stage's carriage, all the way in. Hold the carriage from behind. Crush ribs in the carriage hold the arm with no play: it takes a firm push.
  2. From behind, push the second pin down through the hole in the crossbar, the carriage and the arm. Cut it off flush.

Steps 8 and 9: the cable loop and the dome

  1. Press the cable into the clip in the egg's edge, near the hood's side. Leave about 7 cm of cable between the hood and the clip, in a loose loop. The loop lets the hood float: lift the hood and slide it sideways, the cable must never pull on it.
  2. Put the dome on from behind and press it all round until its five snaps click. The cable runs out through its notch at the clip.

Steps 10 and 11: the base and the tag

  1. Push the egg down into the base's socket, the hood facing out.
  2. Press the tag into its pocket on the front plate, white cells out.

Step 12 and a check: power, and the floating hood

  1. Run the cable along the floor and plug it into the USB-C charger.

Check: the hood floats. Lift it a little and slide it sideways. It springs back to the middle and down onto its stop, cable and all. That's how it finds Microduck's back.

Step 13 and done: the tip in Microduck

  1. On Microduck: push the magnetic tip into the USB-C port on its back. It stays there.

Done! Stand the dock against a wall and stick the base to the floor with removable mounting tape: Microduck pushes the dock when it backs in and pulls it when it leaves.

How it works

  1. Microduck spots the dock's AprilTag, walks up, turns around and sits down.
  2. It scoots backward on its seat until the magnetic tip in its USB-C port meets the adapter in the hood, and stops when USB power appears.
  3. When it is charged, it scoots out against the magnet, stands up and walks off.

The trick is the hood. It rides on a spring stage printed in PLA and hidden inside the egg: it floats up to 15 mm up and 16 mm to either side and springs back to the middle, but toward the egg it barely moves, so the push of docking and the magnet's pull go into the egg, not into the springs. The hood's flared side walls line the duck up, the hood rides up onto its back, and the magnet closes the last millimetre.

Watch it dock in simulation (video on GitHub): it turns, sits, scoots back under the hood, then scoots out, stands up and walks off.

Where it stands

The dock has been printed, assembled and tried by hand. The docking and undocking moves have run in simulation only: there, with the official walk and sit policies and the MicroDock v2's scoot policies, it docked 90 times out of 90 with a normal cable and 90 out of 90 with a stiff one. The scoot policies have not run on a robot yet, and Microduck Studio does not run them. The designer's README has the full trial results and what the simulation leaves out.
