How a Policy Became

Why does a trained duck do what it does? Real training runs answer from their notes and logs. Ten lessons, each with a figure to run.

Who it is for: readers of Book 3, and anyone who wants to know why a trained policy turned out the way it did. The runs are this site's author's, made with duckbatch, an open project that fine-tunes Pollen Robotics' networks in simulation. None of them has run on a physical duck.

How a lesson works: read the left page and commit to a prediction. Then run the figure on the right page one step at a time, and check yourself. Each lesson ends with something to try, notes for whoever is teaching it, and the source of every number.

Lesson 1 · b003, the kernel

The walk that learned to stand

Asked to walk at 0.10 m/s, Pollen's walker moves at 0.002 m/s and duckbatch's 26,254-parameter student at 0.007: both stand still. Asked to turn on the spot at 1.0 rad/s, the student manages 0.211. The first run, b003, set out to close this dead band.

The walking task pays for tracking its command with a kernel, exp(−e2/σ2) of the gap e. At Pollen's width, σ = 0.316 m/s, standing still when asked for 0.10 collects about 90% of the pay. So b003 narrowed σ to 0.1, did the same for turning, and fine-tuned for 1,500 iterations.

The duck stood more: 0.060 m/s when asked for 0.30, against the student's 0.108, and 0.017 rad/s of turning. Yet its logged tracking reward rose all run. At σ = 0.1, managing 0.10 of a 0.30 command earns exp(−4) ≈ 0.018 of the pay, and standing exp(−9) ≈ 0.0001. A first step paid nothing, and other terms still paid for standing.

pay = w · exp(−e2/σ2): the narrower σ, the less a step toward the command pays.

Predict

What did b003's duck do when asked for 0.30 m/s?

Check your prediction

Moved at 0.060, slower than the student. A narrow kernel pays almost nothing until the duck is near the command, so its first steps earned nothing and PPO followed the terms that pay for standing. The log rose because near-zero commands dominate it, and standing meets them.

Try it for real

Chart the kernel yourself: for a 0.30 m/s command, compute exp(−(0.30 − v)2/σ2) for speeds from standing still up to the command, once at σ = 0.316 and once at 0.1. Then read b003 the way it was written: the plan, committed before the run, and the verdict after it. The menu that ran is in duckbatch's menus/ folder, and any menu runs again on Hugging Face Jobs, billed to you, from a copy of duckbatch with your Hugging Face token in HF_TOKEN. Go further has the steps:

uv run duckbatch hf-job menus/b003-dead-band.yaml \
  --run finetune --dataset <you>/duckbatch-records

For teachers and mentors

Show the rising log first and ask whether the run worked. Then show the speeds. Ask what a log would have needed to catch it: tracking at the slow commands on their own, not averaged with the stand commands that standing already meets.

Lesson 2 · b003b, a progress term

Pay for going

b003's note named the fix: keep the kernel's slope and stop paying for standing. b003b put Pollen's widths back and added two progress terms, weight 1.0 each: the share of the commanded speed, and of the commanded turn, that the duck achieves. Standing when told to move earns 0 from them; half the command earns 0.5.

A 100-iteration pilot ran first, to check that the terms run. They did, and it was worse than the student, 0.96 falls a minute against 0.36. The note called that the usual early cost of a warm start, and ran the full 1,500 anyway.

The full run turned on the spot at 0.456 rad/s, 2.2× the student, and reached 0.183 m/s when asked for 0.40, past Pollen's walker. At 0.10 m/s it moved at 0.008, the student's own number. At 0.30 it reached 0.1199 against a line of 0.12, inside the run-to-run noise, and the note recorded a fail.

progress = clip(v · ĉ, 0, |c|) / |c|: pay in proportion to the share of the command achieved, and nothing for standing.

Predict

What did b003b do at 0.10 m/s?

Check your prediction

Moved at 0.008, the student's own number. A reward for going slowly was there, and PPO did not find a slow gait. Above the dead band everything improved, with falls unchanged: progress gave partial walking the slope b003 had removed.

Try it for real

The term is a few lines of duckbatch's rewards.py: the velocity along the command, clipped between zero and the command's size, divided by that size. Chart it beside the tracking kernel for a 0.30 m/s command, tracking at weight 2.0 and progress at 1.0, and compare what the first steps away from standing earn under each. Then read the design, the pilot and the verdict.

For teachers and mentors

The pilot is worth a discussion of its own: it was worse than the student, and the full run went ahead anyway. Ask why that was the honest call. The pass lines were written before either result, and a pilot that only has to run cannot be judged on them.

Lesson 3 · b003c, the practice mix

More practice, more standing

Perhaps the duck rarely practised walking slowly. Pollen's task draws walking commands evenly from ±0.4 m/s forward and ±0.3 sideways, so only about 15% land under 0.15 m/s. b003c changed one thing from b003b: 35% of the practice that was neither standing nor turning in place got a slow command, 0.05 to 0.25 m/s.

The duck stood more. At 0.10 m/s it moved at −0.001. Turning on the spot fell from 0.456 to 0.299 rad/s, and walking at 0.30 m/s from 0.120 to 0.066.

The close note did the sums. At a 0.10 command, the tracking term, weight 2.0, pays exp(−0.01/0.1) ≈ 0.90 of itself for standing still: 1.8 a step, against at most 3.0 for walking perfectly. Walking risks a fall, and the teacher's pull stands near zero. More practice in the band was more practice at a choice standing nearly wins.

At 0.10 m/s, standing earns 2.0 × 0.90 = 1.8 a step; walking it perfectly earns 2.0 + 1.0 = 3.0, before any risk.

Predict

b003c gave the dead band more practice. What did the duck do at 0.10 m/s?

Check your prediction

Stood: −0.001 m/s. The note's verdict: the dead band is not short of practice; standing is too well paid there. And less practice above the band cost b003b's turning and fast walking.

Try it for real

Check the 15% yourself: commands are drawn evenly from a rectangle 0.8 m/s by 0.6 m/s, and the slow ones fall inside a circle of radius 0.15. Then read b003c's design and close notes, and find the sentence that named the next suspect.

For teachers and mentors

Ask the group for a fix before the reveal. More practice is the instinctive answer, and it is wrong here for a reason they can compute: write down what standing and walking each earn at 0.10 m/s, then decide what to change.

  • The command box, 15%, 35% and the slow range: b003c's design note. The speeds, the weight 2.0, exp(−0.01/0.1) ≈ 0.90, 1.8 and 3.0: its close note. 0.8 and 0.6 are the box's sides.

Lesson 4 · What ran

Read the run, not the menu

Each run leaves its records beside its verdict: the menu that asked for it, a TensorBoard log of every reward term and setting at every iteration, a checkpoint every 250 iterations, the exported network and its measurements. The verdict comes from the measurements. The log says what actually ran.

The menus of b003, b003b and b003c each set rel_standing_envs: 0.05: 5% of the practice told to stand still. b003d's design note found that the log's Curriculum/standing_envs read 0.25, at every iteration. Pollen's walking task has a curriculum for that share, which sets it at every reset, so the menu's value never took effect.

A quarter of all practice had been "do not move", in runs meant to teach moving slowly. b003d, b003e and b003f kept 0.25 on purpose, and said so, so that each still changed one thing. b003g, in lesson 7, finally ran the 5%.

A setting is what the log shows ran, not what the menu asked for. Read the logged settings before the results.

Predict

The menus asked for 5%. What did the log say ran?

Check your prediction

25%, at every iteration. The task's curriculum set the share at every reset, over the menu's value. Nothing failed or warned; only the logged value showed it. Turning in place then took a quarter of the envs, which left about 19% standing.

Try it for real

duckbatch's records are a public dataset on Hugging Face, craigm26/duckbatch-records, one folder per job. Download b003's and open its log:

uvx --from huggingface_hub hf download craigm26/duckbatch-records \
  --repo-type dataset --include "jobs/6ab6d30b6b030d633f69210a/*" \
  --local-dir records
uvx tensorboard --logdir records

Find Curriculum/standing_envs before any reward. The fix is in duckbatch's finetune.py, with a comment that tells the story.

For teachers and mentors

Make "what ran?" the first question at every review: the logged settings, then the reward terms, then the results. A setting that silently did nothing is the cheapest bug to find and the most expensive to miss.

Lesson 5 · b003d and b003e, the stride

One change at a time

Each run was planned in a design note before it ran: one change from the run before, the same pass lines, and an expectation to beat. b003d judged tracking relative to the command, so standing at 0.10 m/s paid 0.20 of the term instead of 0.90. Its note said, "I expect it to pass." It moved at 0.004.

Its close note measured why. The tracking error is taken on the duck's instantaneous velocity, and a stride sways the body 0.10 to 0.15 m/s, mostly sideways: more than the command. Under b003d's width, stepping in place, the first move away from standing, paid less than standing. The first step was downhill.

b003e judged the velocity averaged over 0.5 s, where the sway falls to 0.03 to 0.05 m/s. Its design added a weaker bar, 0.02 m/s at 0.10, for whether the idea took at all. It did not: 0.003. But it turned on the spot at 0.525 rad/s, the first run to pass that line.

One change per run, the same lines, and an expectation written down first: then even a failure says something.

Predict

Without the sway, did b003e walk at 0.10 m/s?

Check your prediction

No: 0.003, though it turned better than any run before. A diagnosis can be right and still not be the blocker. With the measured cause gone and the band still shut, the reward was no longer the likeliest suspect, which b003e's design had said in advance would be the conclusion.

Try it for real

duckbatch's stride term keeps the last 0.5 s of each env's velocity, 25 steps at 50 Hz, and refills it at the start of each episode. Read b003d's design and close, then b003e's design and close. The tables of what each term pays along the way, from standing to the command, are the heart of it.

For teachers and mentors

Before each close note, have the group write their own expectation and a number they would bet on. Then compare with the note's. b003d's author expected a pass and wrote so; that sentence is the most useful one in the series.

Lesson 6 · b002 and b003f, the teacher

The teacher's pull

Every run started from duckbatch's student: 61 inputs, two layers of 128, 14 outputs, 26,254 parameters, 7.5× smaller than Pollen's 197,774-parameter walker. It was distilled: the student drove, and the teacher labelled every state its mistakes led to. It tracks within 5% of the teacher, and falls about three times as often.

Fine-tuning kept it tied to the teacher by an anchor: after every PPO update, a pass that pulls the student's actions toward the teacher's, at weight 1.0 on fallen frames, so it keeps getting up, and 0.2 on upright frames commanded to walk at 0.35 m/s or more, or to stand. The dead band was left free.

b003f set the upright weight to 0. Turning on the spot went from 0.456 to 0.806 rad/s, 2.6× Pollen's walker, with recovery at 99.6%. The dead band did not move: 0.004 m/s. The teacher had been a ceiling on turning, not the lock on walking slowly.

anchor = w · mean((π(o) − πteacher(o))2): it keeps what the teacher can do, and holds the student to what it cannot.

Predict

Without the upright pull, what happened to turning?

Check your prediction

It rose to 0.806 rad/s. Every update that found a faster turn had been followed by a pull back toward the teacher, which turns at about 0.31. The pull on fallen frames stayed, so getting up held.

Try it for real

The whole anchor is one loss in duckbatch's finetune.py, run after each PPO update:

loss = (weight[sel] * (pred - target[sel]).pow(2).mean(dim=1)).mean()

A frame counts as fallen when the duck is tilted past 35°. b003's design note sets the weights, and b003f's close note has what letting go cost and bought.

For teachers and mentors

Name the trade before the reveal: the pull on fallen frames is there so the student keeps getting up. Ask what the pull on upright frames protects, and what it might cost. Then show the turning numbers.

Lesson 7 · b003g, the scorecard

Seven lines of eight

b003g made the menus' 5% standing real, and its log read 0.0500 throughout, so the run counted. By the letter of its lines it passed seven of eight: 0.057 m/s at a 0.10 command and 0.038 at 0.05, turning at 0.699 rad/s, 0.136 at 0.30, and the best falls of any fine-tune, 0.22 a minute. Only planar error failed.

Then the note reported what the lines did not ask, as its design had promised. On straight commands the duck turned at about −0.21 rad/s at every speed: it walked clockwise circles, about 0.27 m in radius at 0.10 m/s. Told to stand, it kept stepping, a foot in the air 38% of the time against the student's 8%, and drifted at 0.106 m/s.

The band had opened by stepping all the time on a curve, not by learning a slow straight walk. The verdict: this network is nobody's slow walker. Later runs add two lines, for yaw on a straight command and for drift on a stand.

A pass line measures what it asks and nothing more. For every way a duck could pass and still be wrong, write another line.

Predict

b003g passed its slow-walk lines. What else was true?

Check your prediction

Clockwise circles, and no standing still. Its falls were the series' best, so falling was not the problem. The lines asked for forward speed and nothing about direction or standing, and that is exactly where it gave way.

Try it for real

Read b003g's close note: its table of checkpoints shows the clockwise turn arriving late, at the same time as the slow walking. The note's next step is labelled a hypothesis, not a finding: duckbatch's fine-tune drops Pollen's left-right symmetry augmentation, and nothing else in PPO keeps a gait mirror-symmetric.

For teachers and mentors

Give the group b003g's eight lines and its results, and ask which lines they would add before showing the circles. Then ask what lines Book 3's honest measuring would want for any walk: straightness, standing, falls, and what happens when someone pushes.

Lesson 8 · k001, the kick

Aim, not distance

Pollen's right kick sends the ball 5.064 m, 8.0° off line on average. For shooting, aim matters more than distance. k001 kept Pollen's task and reward and added one term: the ball's speed across the kick line, at weight −12.0, equal and opposite to the 12.0 Pollen pays for its speed along it.

Its lines guarded the trade: 4.0° or straighter, at least 0.95 of Pollen's distance, no more falls, no hopping, and installable with Pollen's own checks. After 1,000 iterations from Pollen's kick it went 3.4° off line, 0.999 of the distance, with falls unchanged. Every line passed.

It also held on the backlash twin, an actuator model it never trained on: 3.8° against Pollen's 8.6°. One number moved that no line covered: peak ball speed, 1.34 to 1.28 m/s. The straighter kick spends less of its speed going sideways.

pay = 12 · valong − 12 · |vacross|: a kick θ off line loses about sin θ of what its speed earns.

Predict

One term against sideways ball speed, 1,000 iterations. How far did the kick go?

Check your prediction

As far: 0.999 of Pollen's, at 3.4° off line. Nudging was the risk the design named, and the distance line was there to catch it. The term charges sideways speed in proportion to forward speed, so slowing down does not escape it.

Try it for real

The term is ball_lateral_speed in duckbatch's rewards.py, and the menu adds it to Pollen's task without touching anything else. Read k001's design and close: Pollen's check_onnx, smoke_run_onnx and validate_manifest all passed on the exported network.

For teachers and mentors

Ask what line would have caught a kick that only nudged the ball, the distance line, and what the result shows no line covered, the peak speed. A good card has a line for the trade you fear, and a report of the numbers you did not plan to watch.

Lesson 9 · The Qwiic port

A new sense on the bus

The walk reads 61 numbers, though the duck senses more, including a camera in its head and, on some ducks, an 8×8 depth sensor. And Pollen has said in its Discord that the duck's board still has an unused Qwiic connector: a small 3.3 V I²C socket, the plug Adafruit calls STEMMA QT.

Pollen's open board, the Robot HAT, has four, J5 to J8. J5 shares the main I²C bus, header pins 3 and 5, with the board's IMU and the audio codec, and Pollen's code reaches the depth sensor "via Stemma J5". The bus runs at 400 kHz, with the codec at 0x18 and the depth sensor, where fitted, at 0x29: a new device needs an address of its own.

A sense reaches behaviour one of two ways. As a command: a program reads it and steers the walk, and no policy changes. Or as an observation: a network trained with more than 61 inputs. Either way, the module's weight changes the body the walk learned.

A sense joins as a command, through a program, or as an observation, through a retrained network.

Predict

What is the quickest way to let a gas sensor steer?

Check your prediction

A program that reads it and steers the walk. The walk's inputs are fixed; it never sees the sensor. A program that turns readings into commands changes no policy. Then weigh the module and measure the walk again: a heavier head is a different body.

Try it for real

On a duck, see what already answers on the bus before you add anything, with the command from Pollen's cheatsheet, and stop the depth sensor's service while you experiment, which the cheatsheet calls safe:

sudo i2cdetect -y -r 3
sudo systemctl stop tofd

Then plug in, run i2cdetect again, and look for the new address. The board's files are open: the Robot HAT, with its schematic in production/.

For teachers and mentors

Draw the two ways in on the board: a program between a sensor and the walk's command is a few lines and changes no network; a new observation means a new network, a manifest whose input count is no longer 61, and robot code that feeds it.

Lesson 10 · Other simulators

The contract travels

Every run in this book trained in mjlab, which joins Isaac Lab's way of writing tasks to MuJoCo Warp's GPU physics. Others are open too: MuJoCo Playground, built on MuJoCo MJX; Isaac Lab itself; and Open Duck Playground, where the Open Duck Mini, a robot Hugging Face and Pollen Robotics sponsored, learns to walk.

The duck cares only about the contract: an ONNX file taking obs[1,61] and returning actions[1,14], 50 times a second, beside a manifest. The robot refuses a file whose manifest claims other sizes. It cannot see a policy that reads its 61 numbers in another order, unit or scale: that file loads, runs, and is a different policy.

So check a port the way duckbatch checks its own export: feed the same inputs to both networks and compare the outputs. b003d's final checkpoint, rebuilt the way the export builds it, matched the exported ONNX with a largest difference of 0 on 256 random inputs.

Any trainer will do if its export keeps the contract, and proves it against a reference on the same inputs.

Predict

Beyond its shapes, what must a port match?

Check your prediction

Every input's and output's order, units and scale. Shapes are what the robot can check. Meaning is what you check, by running the same recorded inputs through the port and a reference.

Try it for real

The manifest that rides beside the file says obs_len 61 and action_len 14, and the robot refuses a policy whose manifest disagrees; Pollen's manifest page has every field. Before a port goes near a duck, rehearse it on a computer the way Book 3's last lesson does, with microduck_rl's infer_policy.py.

For teachers and mentors

Ask what a test bench for a port needs: the reference network, a file of recorded observations, and a tolerance written down before the comparison. It is lesson 5's discipline, applied to a file instead of a run.
