How a Policy Became

Why does a trained duck do what it does? Real training runs answer from their notes and logs. Ten lessons, each with a figure to run.

Who it is for: readers of Book 3, and anyone who wants to know why a trained policy turned out the way it did. The runs are this site's author's, made with duckbatch, an open project that fine-tunes, in simulation, Pollen's kick and a walk distilled from Pollen's. None of them has run on a physical duck.

How a lesson works: read the left page and commit to a prediction. Then run the figure on the right page one step at a time, and check yourself. Each lesson ends with something to try, notes for whoever is teaching it, and the source of every number.

Lesson 1 · b003, the kernel

The walk that learned to stand

Asked to walk at 0.10 m/s, Pollen's walker moves at 0.002 m/s and duckbatch's 26,254-parameter student at 0.007: both stand still. Asked to turn on the spot at 1.0 rad/s, the student manages 0.211. The first fine-tune, b003, set out to close this dead band.

The walking task pays for tracking its command with a kernel, exp(−e2/σ2) of the gap e. At Pollen's width, σ = 0.316 m/s, standing still when asked for 0.10 collects about 90% of the pay. So b003 narrowed σ to 0.1, narrowed turning's width too, and fine-tuned for 1,500 iterations.

The duck stood more: 0.060 m/s when asked for 0.30, against the student's 0.108, and 0.017 rad/s of turning. Yet its logged tracking reward rose over the run. At σ = 0.1, managing 0.10 of a 0.30 command earns exp(−4) ≈ 0.018 of the pay, and standing exp(−9) ≈ 0.0001. A first step paid nothing, and other terms still paid for standing.

pay = w · exp(−e2/σ2): the narrower σ, the less a step toward the command pays.

Predict

What did b003's duck do when asked for 0.30 m/s?

Check your prediction

Moved at 0.060, slower than the student. A narrow kernel pays almost nothing until the duck is near the command, so its first steps earned nothing and PPO followed the terms that pay for standing. The close note's reading of the rising log: near-zero commands dominate it, and standing meets them.

Try it for real

Chart the kernel yourself: for a 0.30 m/s command, compute exp(−(0.30 − v)2/σ2) for speeds from standing still up to the command, once at σ = 0.316 and once at 0.1. Then read b003 the way it was written: the plan, committed before the run, and the verdict after it. The menu that ran is in duckbatch's menus/ folder, and any menu runs again on Hugging Face Jobs, billed to you, from a copy of duckbatch with your Hugging Face token in HF_TOKEN. Go further has the steps:

uv run duckbatch hf-job menus/b003-dead-band.yaml \
  --run finetune --dataset <you>/duckbatch-records

For teachers and mentors

Show the rising log first and ask whether the run worked. Then show the speeds. Ask what a log would have needed to catch it: tracking at the slow commands on their own, not averaged with the stand commands that standing already meets.

Lesson 2 · b003b, a progress term

Pay for going

b003's note named the fix: keep the kernel's slope and stop paying for standing. b003b put Pollen's widths back and added two progress terms, weight 1.0 each: the share of the commanded speed, and of the commanded turn, that the duck achieves. Standing when told to move earns 0 from them; half the command earns 0.5.

A 100-iteration pilot ran first, to check that the terms run. They did, and it was worse than the student, 0.96 falls a minute against 0.36. The note called that the usual early cost of a warm start, though not yet known to be only that, and the pre-registered full 1,500 went ahead.

The full run turned on the spot at 0.456 rad/s, 2.2× the student, and reached 0.183 m/s when asked for 0.40, past Pollen's walker. At 0.10 m/s it moved at 0.008, against the student's 0.007: no change. At 0.30 it reached 0.1199 against a line of 0.12, inside the run-to-run noise, and the note recorded a fail.

progress = clip(v · ĉ, 0, |c|) / |c|: pay in proportion to the share of the command achieved, and nothing for standing.

Predict

What did b003b do at 0.10 m/s?

Check your prediction

Moved at 0.008, about the student's speed. A reward for going slowly was there, and PPO did not find a slow gait. Above the dead band everything improved, with falls unchanged. The note credits the progress terms, though no run tried Pollen's widths without them.

Try it for real

The term is a few lines of duckbatch's rewards.py: the velocity along the command, clipped between zero and the command's size, divided by that size. Chart it beside the tracking kernel for a 0.30 m/s command, tracking at weight 2.0 and progress at 1.0, and compare what the first steps away from standing earn under each. Then read the design, the pilot and the verdict.

For teachers and mentors

The pilot is worth a discussion of its own: it was worse than the student, and the full run went ahead anyway. Ask why that was the honest call. The pass lines were written before either result, and a pilot that only has to run cannot be judged on them.

  • The weights and what half the command earns: b003b's design note. 100 iterations and the falls: its pilot note. The speeds, 1,500 iterations, 2.2×, 0.1199 and the line: its close note.

Lesson 3 · b003c, the practice mix

More practice, more standing

Perhaps the duck rarely practised walking slowly. Pollen's task draws most walking commands evenly from ±0.4 m/s forward and ±0.3 sideways, and a fifth straight ahead at 0.3 or more, so only about 12% land under 0.15 m/s. b003c changed one thing from b003b: 35% of the envs neither standing nor turning in place got a slow command, 0.05 to 0.25 m/s.

The duck stood more. At 0.10 m/s it moved at −0.001. Turning on the spot fell from 0.456 to 0.299 rad/s, and walking at 0.30 m/s from 0.120 to 0.066.

The close note did the sums. At a 0.10 command, the tracking term, weight 2.0, pays exp(−0.01/0.1) ≈ 0.90 of itself for standing still: 1.8 a step, against at most 3.0 for walking perfectly. More practice in the band was more practice at a choice standing nearly wins.

At 0.10 m/s, standing earns 2.0 × 0.90 = 1.8 a step; walking it perfectly earns 2.0 + 1.0 = 3.0, before any risk.

Predict

b003c gave the dead band more practice. What did the duck do at 0.10 m/s?

Check your prediction

Stood: −0.001 m/s. The note's verdict: the dead band is not short of practice; standing is too well paid there. And b003b's turning and fast walking were lost, which the note put down, as far as its numbers went, to less practice above the band.

Try it for real

Check the 12% yourself: the boxed commands are drawn evenly over a rectangle 0.8 m/s by 0.6 m/s, the slow ones fall inside a circle of radius 0.15, and the fifth sent straight ahead are never slow. Then read b003c's design and close notes, and find the sentence that named the next suspect.

For teachers and mentors

Ask the group for a fix before the reveal. More practice is the instinctive answer, and it is wrong here for a reason they can compute: write down what standing and walking each earn at 0.10 m/s, then decide what to change.

Where these numbers come from

  • The command box, 35% and the slow range: b003c's design note, which put the slow share at 15%, without the fifth. That fifth, straight ahead at 0.3 or more: mjlab's walking task, which Pollen's builds on. About 12% is the circle's share of the box, for the rest. The speeds, the weight 2.0, exp(−0.01/0.1) ≈ 0.90, 1.8 and 3.0: its close note. 0.8 and 0.6 are the box's sides.

Lesson 4 · What ran

Read the run, not the menu

Each run leaves its records beside its verdict: the menu that asked for it, a TensorBoard log of every reward term and of the settings the curriculum manages, a checkpoint every 250 iterations, and the exported network with its measurements. Where the log records a setting, it says what actually ran.

The menus of b003, b003b and b003c each set rel_standing_envs: 0.05: 5% of the practice told to stand still. b003d's design note found that the log's Curriculum/standing_envs read 0.25, at every iteration. Pollen's walking task has a curriculum for that share, which sets it at every reset, so the menu's value never took effect.

About a fifth of all practice had been "do not move", in runs meant to teach moving slowly. b003d, b003e and b003f kept 0.25 on purpose, and said so, so that each still changed one thing. b003g, in lesson 7, finally ran the 5%.

Where the log records a setting, the log is what ran, not the menu. Read those values before the results.

Predict

The menus asked for 5%. What did the log say ran?

Check your prediction

25%, at every iteration. The task's curriculum set the share at every reset, over the menu's value. Nothing failed, and the menu still said 5%; the log is where it showed. Turning in place then took a quarter of the envs, which left about 19% standing.

Try it for real

duckbatch's records are a public dataset on Hugging Face, craigm26/duckbatch-records, one folder per job. Download b003's and open its log:

uvx --from huggingface_hub hf download craigm26/duckbatch-records \
  --repo-type dataset --include "jobs/6ab6d30b6b030d633f69210a/*" \
  --local-dir records
uvx tensorboard --logdir records/jobs

Find Curriculum/standing_envs before any reward. The fix is in duckbatch's finetune.py, with a comment that tells the story.

For teachers and mentors

Make "what ran?" the first question at every review: the logged settings, then the reward terms, then the results. A setting that silently did nothing is the cheapest bug to find and the most expensive to miss.

Lesson 5 · b003d and b003e, the stride

One change at a time

From b003c on, each run was planned in a design note first: one change from a named earlier run, usually b003b, the best so far; the series' pass lines; and an expectation to beat. b003d judged tracking relative to the command, so standing at 0.10 m/s paid 0.20 of the term instead of 0.90. Its note said, "I expect it to pass." It moved at 0.004.

Its close note found a candidate. The tracking error is taken on the duck's instantaneous velocity, and a stride sways the body 0.10 to 0.15 m/s, mostly sideways: more than the command. Under b003d's width, stepping in place, the first move away from standing, paid less than standing. The first step was downhill.

b003e judged the velocity averaged over 0.5 s, where the sway falls to 0.03 to 0.05 m/s. Its design added a weaker bar, 0.02 m/s at 0.10, for whether the idea took at all. It did not: 0.003. But it turned on the spot at 0.525 rad/s, the first run to pass that line.

One change per run, the same lines, and an expectation written down first: then even a failure says something.

Predict

Without the sway, did b003e walk at 0.10 m/s?

Check your prediction

No: 0.003, though it turned better than any run before. A diagnosis can be right and still not be the blocker. With the dip gone and the band still shut, the reward was no longer the likeliest suspect, as b003e's design had said in advance.

Try it for real

duckbatch's stride term keeps the last 0.5 s of each env's velocity, 25 steps at 50 Hz, and refills it at the start of each episode. Read b003d's design and close, then b003e's design and close. The tables of what each term pays along the way, from standing to the command, are the heart of it.

For teachers and mentors

Before each close note, have the group write their own expectation and a number they would bet on. Then compare with the note's. b003d's author expected a pass and wrote so; that sentence is the most useful one in the series. Then point out that the sway was measured on faster walkers, and the note itself says a real slow gait's might be smaller: ask what that does to the argument.

Lesson 6 · b002 and b003f, the teacher

The teacher's pull

Every walking run started from duckbatch's student: 61 inputs, two layers of 128, 14 outputs, 26,254 parameters, 7.5× smaller than Pollen's 197,774-parameter walker. It was distilled: the student drove, and the teacher labelled every state its mistakes led to. It tracks speed within 5% of the teacher and turning within 11%, and falls about three times as often under pushes.

Fine-tuning kept it tied to the teacher by an anchor: after every PPO update, a pass that pulls the student's actions toward the teacher's, at weight 1.0 on fallen frames and 0.2 on upright frames commanded to walk at 0.35 m/s or more, or to stand. The dead band was left free.

b003f set the upright weight to 0. Turning on the spot went from 0.456 to 0.806 rad/s, 2.6× Pollen's walker, with recovery at 99.6%. The dead band did not move: 0.004 m/s. The note's reading: the teacher had been a ceiling on turning, not the lock on walking slowly.

anchor = w · mean((π(o) − πteacher(o))2): it keeps the teacher's skills, and its limits.

Predict

Without the upright pull, what happened to turning?

Check your prediction

It rose to 0.806 rad/s. The pull never touched a turn-in-place frame, but it held the shared network to the teacher on standing and fast-walking frames, and the teacher turns at about 0.31. The pull on fallen frames stayed, so getting up held.

Try it for real

The whole anchor is one loss in duckbatch's finetune.py, run after each PPO update:

loss = (weight[sel] * (pred - target[sel]).pow(2).mean(dim=1)).mean()

A frame counts as fallen when the duck is tilted past 35°. b003's design note sets the weights, and b003f's close note has what letting go cost and bought.

For teachers and mentors

Name the trade before the reveal: the pull on fallen frames is there so the student keeps getting up. Ask what the pull on upright frames protects, and what it might cost. Then show the turning numbers.

Lesson 7 · b003g, the scorecard

Seven lines of eight

b003g made the menus' 5% standing real, and its log read 0.0500 throughout, so the run counted. By the letter of its lines it passed seven of eight: 0.057 m/s at a 0.10 command and 0.038 at 0.05, turning at 0.699 rad/s, 0.136 at 0.30, and the best falls of any fine-tune, 0.22 a minute. Only planar error failed.

Then the note reported what the lines did not ask. On straight commands the duck turned at about −0.21 rad/s at every speed: it walked clockwise circles, about 0.27 m in radius at 0.10 m/s. Told to stand, it kept stepping, a foot in the air 38% of the time against the student's 8%, and drifted at 0.106 m/s.

The band had opened by stepping all the time on a curve, not by learning a slow straight walk. The verdict: this network is nobody's slow walker. Later runs are to add two lines, for yaw on a straight command and for drift on a stand.

A pass line measures what it asks and nothing more. For every way a duck could pass and still be wrong, write another line.

Predict

b003g passed its slow-walk lines. What else was true?

Check your prediction

Clockwise circles, and no standing still. Its falls were the series' best, so falling was not the problem. The speed lines asked only for forward speed. Nothing asked about yaw on a straight command, and standing showed only inside the averaged planar error, the one line that failed.

Try it for real

Read b003g's close note: its table of checkpoints shows the clockwise turn arriving late, at the same time as the slow walking. The note's next step is labelled a hypothesis, not a finding: nothing in duckbatch's PPO keeps a gait mirror-symmetric. Its premise, that Pollen trains this walk with a symmetry term, does not hold at the code the runs used: Pollen's walking task sets symmetry_cfg=None.

For teachers and mentors

Give the group b003g's eight lines and its results, and ask which lines they would add before showing the circles. Then ask what lines Book 3's honest measuring would want for any walk: straightness, standing, falls, and what happens when someone pushes.

Lesson 8 · k001, the kick

Aim, not distance

Pollen's right kick sends the ball 5.064 m, 8.0° off line on average. For shooting, aim matters more than distance. k001 kept Pollen's task and reward and added one term: the ball's speed across the kick line, at weight −12.0, the rate Pollen pays for speed along it, up to 1.0 m/s.

Its lines guarded the trade: 4.0° or straighter, at least 0.95 of Pollen's distance, falls and hopping held, and installable with Pollen's own checks. After 1,000 iterations from Pollen's kick it went 3.4° off line, 0.999 of the distance, with falls unchanged. Every line passed.

It also held on the backlash twin, an actuator model it never trained on: 3.7° against Pollen's 8.6°. One number moved that no line covered: the ball's peak speed along the line, 1.34 to 1.28 m/s. The notes do not test why.

pay = 12 · min(valong, 1.0) − 4 · max(0, valong − 1.0) − 12 · |vacross|: speed across always costs; along pays only up to 1.0 m/s.

Predict

One term against sideways ball speed, 1,000 iterations. How far did the kick go?

Check your prediction

As far: 0.999 of Pollen's, at 3.4° off line. Nudging was the risk the design named, and the distance line was there to catch it. It did not have to: the kick kept its distance and lost most of its angle.

Try it for real

The term is ball_lateral_speed in duckbatch's rewards.py, and the menu adds it to Pollen's task without touching anything else. Read k001's design and close: Pollen's check_onnx, smoke_run_onnx and validate_manifest all passed on the exported network.

For teachers and mentors

Ask what line would have caught a kick that only nudged the ball, the distance line, and what the result shows no line covered, the peak speed. Then read Pollen's kick reward: speed along the line pays only up to 1.0 m/s, and each m/s above it costs 4. A good card has a line for the trade you fear, and a report of the numbers you did not plan to watch.

Lesson 9 · The Qwiic port

A new sense on the bus

The walk reads 61 numbers, though the duck senses more: a camera in its head and, on some ducks, an 8×8 depth sensor. Pollen has said in its Discord that the duck's board has an unused Qwiic connector: a 3.3 V I²C socket, the plug Adafruit calls STEMMA QT.

Pollen's open board, the Robot HAT, has four, J5 to J8. J5 shares the main I²C bus, header pins 3 and 5, with the board's IMU and audio codec; the others' buses depend on the computer. On Zero 3W ducks the depth sensor, where fitted, plugs into J5, on a 400 kHz bus with the codec at 0x18 and the sensor at 0x29: a new device needs its own address.

A sense reaches behaviour two ways. As a command: a program reads it and steers the walk, changing no policy. Or as an observation: a network trained with more than 61 inputs. Either way, the module's weight changes the body the walk learned.

A sense joins as a command, through a program, or as an observation, through retraining.

Predict

What is the quickest way to let a gas sensor steer?

Check your prediction

A program that reads it and steers the walk. The walk's inputs are fixed; it never sees the sensor. A program that turns readings into commands changes no policy. Then weigh the module and measure the walk again: a heavier head is a different body.

Try it for real

On a duck, see what already answers on the bus before you add anything, with the command from Pollen's cheatsheet, and stop the depth sensor's service while you experiment, which the cheatsheet calls safe:

sudo i2cdetect -y -r 3
sudo systemctl stop tofd

Then switch the duck off, plug in, switch it on, and stop tofd again, since it starts at boot. Run i2cdetect again and look for the new address; if it is not there, i2cdetect -l lists every bus. The board's files are open: the Robot HAT, with its schematic in production/.

For teachers and mentors

Draw the two ways in on the board: a program between a sensor and the walk's command is a few lines and changes no network; a new observation means a new network, a manifest whose input count is no longer 61, and robot code that feeds it.

Lesson 10 · Other simulators

The contract travels

Every run in this book trained in mjlab, which joins Isaac Lab's way of writing tasks to MuJoCo Warp's GPU physics. Others are open: MuJoCo Playground, on MuJoCo MJX; Isaac Lab itself; and Open Duck Playground, for the Open Duck Mini, which Hugging Face and Pollen sponsored.

The duck checks only the contract: an ONNX file taking obs[1,61] and returning actions[1,14], 50 times a second, usually beside a manifest. It refuses other sizes, in the file or its manifest. It cannot see a policy that reads its 61 numbers in another order, unit or scale: it loads, runs, and is a different policy.

So check a port twice. The export: the trained network and its file must agree on the same inputs, as b003d's did, a largest difference of 0 on 256 random inputs. The meaning: your trainer and Pollen's must build the same 61 numbers from the same recorded states. A swapped input passes the first and fails the second.

Prove the file and the 61 numbers, not just the shapes.

Predict

Beyond its shapes, what must a port match?

Check your prediction

Every input's and output's order, units and scale. Shapes are what the robot can check. Meaning is what you check: the same recorded states, turned into 61 numbers by your trainer and by Pollen's, must agree.

Try it for real

The manifest that rides beside the file says obs_len 61 and action_len 14, and the robot refuses a policy whose manifest disagrees; Pollen's manifest page has every field. Before a port goes near a duck, rehearse it on a computer the way Book 3's last lesson does, with microduck_rl's infer_policy.py.

For teachers and mentors

Ask what a test bench for a port needs: recorded states, the 61 numbers Pollen's trainer makes from them, and a tolerance written down before the comparison. Pollen's own robot design wants the same check, because a wrong index in the observation "does not fail loudly". It is lesson 5's discipline, applied to a file instead of a run.
