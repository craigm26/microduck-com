The Duck Who Stayed

Chapter 1: A Box From the Post Office

For children 5 to 8. A grown-up reads it to a child of 5 or 6. A child of about 7 reads it alone.

First draft. More chapters are coming.

The box

On the very last hot day of summer, the truck came home from the post office with a box.

A box! With a name on it!

The name was not Wren. Wren felt this was a mistake.

“It has my name on it,” said Aunt Mo.

“You could share your name,” said Wren.

And what was inside?

Not yet

“Can we open it now?”

“Not yet.”

“Now?”

“Not yet.”

“How about now?”

Aunt Mo cut the tape. Wren stood on a chair. Foam. More foam. Foam, foam, foam. Then up poked one small flat beak.

“Pipped,” said Grandpa Sol. “Same as a chick coming through its shell.”

“Pip!” said Wren, quickly, before anyone had a different idea.

The goat

Out came Pip, all at once, no taller than Grandpa Sol’s boot.

“Does it lay?” asked Grandpa Sol.

“No,” said Aunt Mo.

“Then what’s it for?”

“I don’t know yet.”

Grandpa Sol nodded. He once had a goat like that.

Wren’s finger crept toward the green button.

“Not yet,” said Aunt Mo, and out came the manual.

The manual

Aunt Mo read page one. Wren ate an apple.

Page two. Wren ate an apple.

Page three. Wren ran out of apples.

“Keep Pip away from water,” read Aunt Mo.

“A duck,” said Grandpa Sol, “that keeps away from water.”

Nobody had anything to add.

“What does that page say?”

“It says, This page is blank.”

Wren thought about that for some time.

Hot soup

“All right,” said Aunt Mo. “Now.”

Wren pressed once. Whirr. Up stood Pip.

Wren pressed twice. Tick, tick, tick.

Pip walked! It walked like someone carrying hot soup.

Wren ran ahead and knelt with her arms out.

“Come on, Pip! Come on!”

Tick, tick, tick.

A little to one side.

A little more to one side.

Past her hand...

CLONK

CLONK!

Over went Pip, flat on its side, with both legs still going.

“It’s broken!” said Wren.

Aunt Mo held up one finger.

“Wait.”

Tuck, push, roll, up

The legs stopped.

Tuck.

Push.

Roll.

Up!

Whirr. Tick, tick, tick.

And Pip walked into the very same chair leg.

CLONK!

Wren dragged the chair way over there.

Tuck.

Push.

Roll.

Up!

Whirr. Tick, tick, tick.

And Pip walked into a different leg.

CLONK!

That we can know

Aunt Mo pressed stop. Page twelve said: Pip does not look. Pip just goes.

“Pip doesn’t like that chair,” said Wren.

“We can’t know that,” said Aunt Mo.

“It went CLONK three times!”

“That we can know.”

Grandpa Sol went back to the hen house, looking pleased about something.

The green book

Pip went on the shelf to charge.

“Can I show the hens?”

“Not yet.”

Out came a thin green book.

“This is blank,” said Wren.

“It’s a log. For what Pip did.”

“Can I put that Pip was mad?”

“Did you see mad?”

Wren looked. One small light, and nothing else.

She went to find a pencil.

Wren's log

WREN’S LOG. Machine: Pip. Day 1.

Look: Pip walked into the chair leg. Two times.

Tried: Moved the chair. Pip walked into another leg. One time.

Told: Aunt Mo. Grandpa Sol. They were there.

Chair leg: fine. I checked.

Tomorrow: hens. They have not been told.

Not yet.
