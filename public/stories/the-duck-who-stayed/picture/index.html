The Duck Who Stayed

Chapter 1: A Box From the Post Office

For children 5 to 8. A grown-up reads it to a child of 5 or 6. A child of about 7 reads it alone.

First draft. More chapters are coming.

The box

On the last hot day of summer, the truck came home with a box.

A box! With a name on it!

The name was not Wren. It was Aunt Mo. Wren felt this was a mistake.

Tam from the next farm came too. Tam counted things.

Nod was the robot on the table: one head, two antennas, no hands. It nodded at everything.

“That box,” said Nod, “is a ham.”

Not yet

“Can we open it now?”

“Not yet.”

“Now?”

“Not yet.”

“How about now?”

Aunt Mo cut the tape. Foam. More foam. Foam, foam, foam. Then up poked one small flat beak.

“Ham with a beak,” said Nod.

“Pipped,” said Grandpa Sol. “Like a chick through its shell.”

“Pip!” said Wren, quickly, before anyone had a different idea.

The goat

Aunt Mo lifted Pip to the floor. It stood one boot high.

“Does it lay?” asked Grandpa Sol.

“No,” said Aunt Mo.

“Then what’s it for?”

“I don’t know yet.”

Grandpa Sol nodded. He once had a goat like that.

Nod nodded too.

Wren’s finger crept toward the green button.

“Not yet,” said Aunt Mo.

The manual

Aunt Mo read the manual. Page one. Wren ate an apple.

Page two. Wren ate an apple.

Page three. Wren ran out of apples.

“Keep Pip away from water,” read Aunt Mo.

“A duck,” said Grandpa Sol, “that keeps away from water.”

“What’s that page say?”

“It says, This page is blank.”

Wren thought about that. Nod nodded.

Hot soup

“All right,” said Aunt Mo. “Now.”

Wren pressed once. Whirr. Up stood Pip.

Wak-wak?

“That means ham,” said Nod.

Wren pressed twice. Tick, tick, tick.

Pip walked like someone carrying hot soup.

“This walk is the one!” said Nod.

A little to one side. Past Wren’s hand...

ACK! ACK! ACK!

“That means all is well,” said Nod.

Was it?

CLONK

CLONK!

Over went Pip, flat on its side, with both legs still going.

Up on the table, Nod could not see the floor. It nodded anyway.

“That’s one,” said Tam.

“It’s broken!” said Wren.

Aunt Mo held up one finger.

“Wait.”

Tuck, push, roll, up

Tuck.

Push.

Roll.

Up!

Prrip!

“That means this is the one!” said Nod.

Tick, tick, tick.

And Pip walked into the same leg.

ACK! ACK! ACK!

CLONK!

“That’s two.”

Wren dragged the chair way over there.

Tuck.

Push.

Roll.

Up!

“This is the one!”

Tick, tick, tick.

And Pip walked into a different leg.

ACK! ACK! ACK!

CLONK!

“That’s three.”

We can know that

Aunt Mo scooped up Pip.

Wheeeeak!

“That means Pip loves that chair,” said Nod.

“Pip doesn’t like that chair,” said Wren.

“We can’t know that,” said Aunt Mo.

Page twelve of the manual said: Pip does not look. Pip just goes.

“It went CLONK three times!”

“Yes. We can know that.”

“Three,” said Nod.

Tam had said it first.

The green book

Pip went on the shelf to charge.

Cooo-ak.

“That means mad,” said Nod.

“Can I show the hens?”

“Not yet.”

Out came a green book.

“This is blank,” said Wren.

“It’s a log. For what Pip did.”

“Can I put that Pip was mad?”

“Did you see mad?”

Wren looked. One small light.

She went to find a pencil.

Wren's log

WREN’S LOG. Machine: Pip. Day 1.

Look: Pip walked into the chair leg. Two times.

Tried: Moved the chair. Pip walked into another leg. One time.

Heard: ACK! ACK! ACK! before every CLONK.

Told: Tam said three. Two and one is three.

Nod says that means all is well. I did not write that down.

Chair leg: fine. I checked.

Tomorrow: hens. They have not been told.

Not yet.
