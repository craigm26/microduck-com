About this book

The Duck Who Stayed is a made-up story about Wren, her family's farm and Pip, a small walking robot. This is a first draft. Chapter 1 is here, in two editions with the same words: a flat picture book and a 3-D book with pages that turn. It has not yet been tested with children, and more chapters are coming.

The story is invented. It is written for children 5 to 8: a grown-up reads it to a child of 5 or 6, and a child of about 7 reads it alone.

A note for parents

The book is meant to be read aloud. Pause before each "Not yet" and each CLONK, and children may start saying them with you.

Pip has no words, only five quacks, each spelled the same way every time so a child can learn it and make it. The story never says what they mean. Nod says, and Nod is the one to catch.

Wren says what she thinks Pip feels, and Aunt Mo answers, "We can't know that." A child who says Pip is glad has not got anything wrong. It is a good moment to ask what Pip actually did.

The question below has no single right answer. It is there to be talked about.

If a child is near a real robot, a grown-up stays close by, and the child stays out of its path while it runs. Aunt Mo does the same in the story.

A question for the grown-up

Wren thinks Pip doesn't like that chair. Nod thinks Pip loves it. What did Pip do that made them think so? Nod said ACK! ACK! ACK! means all is well. What happened right after? Have you ever guessed what someone felt, and guessed wrong?

Contact

These pages

The book's home and this page run no scripts. The two editions of chapter 1 run this site's own scripts to turn the pages, and both still read with scripts off. No page of this book loads anything from any other site.
