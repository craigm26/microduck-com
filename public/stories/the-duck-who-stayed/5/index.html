Chapter 5 Bump!

First draft. More chapters are coming.

In the first cool week of autumn, Grandpa Sol was turning the onions to dry.

Wren had told the hens that Pip would go through the yard gate before supper. She had chalked a square on the far side to prove it. Tam had to be home by supper and had stayed to see. Aunt Mo sat on a crate by the pump, cleaning a wheel.

Pip stood on a flat stone, set level with the ground, close by the gate. Wren knelt in front of it, nose to beak, and held out her hand. Tam passed the cards, and she laid them in a row beside the stone.

Start. Walk forward.

The gate was on Wren's right. "Turn right," she said.

Tam held on to the card. "Are you sure that's..."

"Hand it here," said Wren.

Walk forward. Walk forward. End.

Wren shuffled clear. Pip's head turned slowly along the row. Tick, tick, tick. Pip walked forward. Pip turned. Pip turned away from the gate.

"No," said Wren. "Pip. Gate."

Pip carried its soup to the woodpile on the other side. It walked into a log.

Bump!

Pip tipped over and stuck there, wedged, legs pushing. Aunt Mo got there in four steps and pressed stop.

"Stop first," she said. "Legs pushing on a log get hot. Now we look."

Wren was already looking at Tam. "You gave me the wrong card."

Tam put the cards down. "I gave you the one you asked for."

Tam went and sat on the back step, facing the other way.

"Let's find the fault," said Aunt Mo.

Wren got out the green book. She wrote a list called Who Did It. Pip. The stone. Drift. Tam. She thought, then added Chair leg.

Aunt Mo read it. "The chair's in the kitchen."

Wren went to the window and checked. It was.

"Pip stays off for now," said Aunt Mo. "What can you test without Pip?"

Wren looked at the row of cards. She looked at her own two feet.

She stood on the stone, facing the way Pip had faced. Walk forward. She took one duck step, heel to toe. Turn right, and she turned right. Walk forward, walk forward.

Her shin met the log.

"Bump," said Wren quietly.

She went back and did it again, slower. Same log.

Back on the stone, the gate was on her left. She went and knelt where she had knelt, facing the stone. Now the gate was on her right. Stone, left. Knees, right. Grandpa Sol stopped turning onions to watch. He didn't ask.

Wren picked up the Turn right card and held it for quite a long time. Nobody had put it there but her.

"Pip did what I said."

"Exactly what you said," said Aunt Mo. "That's a bug."

"So whose fault is it?"

Aunt Mo turned the card over, as if the answer might be on the back. "That's a good question," she said.

"Is that the answer?"

"No."

It was eleven steps to where Tam sat. Wren counted.

"It was my card," she said.

"I know," said Tam.

"You knew?"

"You were facing Pip."

"Why didn't you say?"

"I started to."

Wren thought about that. She sat down. They watched a hen for a bit.

Then Tam got up, found Turn left in the pile, and held it out. Wren put it in the row. She changed that one card and no others.

Aunt Mo lifted Pip clear and laid it on its side on flat ground. She pressed the green button.

Nothing happened. Then Pip tucked one foot in. It pushed. It rolled, and rocked, and rolled again. It stood upright. Whirr, tick.

"Pip's glad," said Wren.

"We can't know that."

"Pip looks glad."

"Pip looks upright," said Aunt Mo.

They set Pip on the stone. Pip read the row. It walked, it turned left, and it went through the gate on a slant. It scraped one post on the way. It stopped two hands short of the chalk square.

Tam cheered. Wren looked at the square.

"Nearly," she said. She did not say it nicely.

Tam was very late for supper by then. At the gate Tam turned and started to say something. Wren was still looking at the square, so Tam ran.

WREN'S LOG. Machine: Pip. Day 23.

  • Look: Pip hit a log. I put it in the log.
  • Tried: Walked the cards myself. Changed one card.
  • Told: Aunt Mo. Tam. Tam already knew.
  • Gate: through. Square: nearly.
  • Chair leg: not involved this time.

Real robots. A sensor measures tilt. When a small walking robot tips too far, a program runs the getting-up moves. A bug is a mistake in the instructions. A machine follows its instructions even when they are wrong.

Big Question (back page). Whose fault was it? Could it be more than one person's, or nobody's?

Try It: Find the bug. You need the Duck Cards at the back of this book, a friend and a big book. Put the big book on the floor, a few duck steps away and off to one side, so the row needs a turn. One of you is the planner and one is the walker. The walker looks away. The planner lays a row from Start to End that reaches the big book, then swaps one card for a wrong one. The walker walks the row, heel to toe. If the next step would reach anything, stop and say "Bump." Now the walker finds the fault. Stand facing the way the duck faces. Change one card, then walk it again. Tell what you found. Swap.
