Chapter 1 A Box From the Post Office

First draft. More chapters are coming.

At the end of summer, Grandpa Sol was mending the hen-house door.

The truck came back from the post office with a box. The box had Aunt Mo's name on it, which Wren felt was a mistake. She wanted to open it herself, and she wanted to do it now.

"It has my name on it," said Aunt Mo.

"You could share your name," said Wren.

Aunt Mo set the box on the kitchen table and cut the tape. Wren stood on a chair to see. There was foam, and more foam. Then a small flat beak poked through.

Grandpa Sol came in with his hammer. "It's pipped," he said.

"What's pipped?"

"A chick's first hole in the shell. That's a pip."

Wren said that was the name, quickly, before anyone had a different idea.

Pip came out of the foam all at once. It was no taller than Grandpa Sol's boot.

"Does it lay?" asked Grandpa Sol.

"No," said Aunt Mo.

"Then what's it for?"

"I don't know yet."

Grandpa Sol nodded. He said he once had a goat like that.

Wren put her finger on the green button.

"Not yet," said Aunt Mo, and she took out the manual.

Aunt Mo read page one and page two. Wren ate an apple. Aunt Mo read all about the battery.

"Keep Pip away from water," she read out.

"A duck," said Grandpa Sol, "that keeps away from water."

Nobody had anything to add to that.

Wren ate another apple, slower. Aunt Mo stopped near the back and frowned at one page.

"What does that page say?" asked Wren.

"It says, This page is blank."

Wren thought about that for some time.

"All right," said Aunt Mo at last. "Floor clear. One press to stand, two to walk."

Wren pressed. Whirr. Pip's legs unfolded. Wren pressed twice. Tick, tick, tick.

Pip walked. It walked like someone carrying hot soup. Wren ran to the far end of the kitchen and knelt with her arms out.

"Come on, Pip! Come on!"

Pip came on. It came on a little to one side. It went past her left hand and straight into the leg of a chair.

Clonk.

Pip tipped over. It lay on its side with both legs still going.

"It's broken!" said Wren.

Aunt Mo held up one finger. "Wait."

The legs stopped. Pip tucked one foot in. It pushed. It rolled. It stood up, whirr, tick, and walked into the same chair leg.

Clonk.

Aunt Mo turned to page twelve and read it out. "Pip does not look out for things in its way. Pip keeps walking until you stop it."

Wren dragged the chair over a bit. Pip got up again and walked into a different leg.

Clonk.

Grandpa Sol went back to his door. He seemed pleased about something.

"Pip doesn't like that chair," said Wren.

"We can't know that," said Aunt Mo.

"It hit it three times."

"That we can know."

Aunt Mo pressed stop. She carried Pip to the shelf by the back door and plugged it in.

"Can I do the button again? Can I show the hens?"

"Tomorrow, with me. Pip has to charge."

Wren had waited all day for that button. She had pressed it three times. It did not seem like very many.

Aunt Mo took a thin green book from the drawer and held it out. Wren opened it. She turned a page, and another.

"This is blank," she said.

"It's a log," said Aunt Mo. "Every machine on this farm has one. It's where you write what a machine did."

She drew three words at the top of the first page. Look. Tried. Told.

"What you saw. What you did about it. Who you told."

"Can I put that Pip was cross?"

"Did you see cross?"

Wren looked across at the shelf. Pip's small light blinked. Tick, went Pip, and then nothing else.

She went to find a pencil.

WREN'S LOG. Machine: Pip. Day 1.

  • Look: Pip walked into the chair leg. Two times.
  • Tried: Moved the chair. Pip found another leg.
  • Told: Aunt Mo. Grandpa Sol. They were there.
  • Chair leg: fine. I checked.
  • Tomorrow: hens. They have not been told.

Real robots. A walking robot moves small motors to stay up on two legs. It goes around a chair only if someone has told it how. A log records what a machine did and what people did about it.

Big Question (back page). Is Pip a friend or a tool? What makes you say so?

Try It: Be the duck. You need the Duck Cards at the back of this book, a friend, a chair and some clear floor. Put the chair a few duck steps away. Plan a row from Start to End that gets past the chair. One of you is the caller, who reads the cards one at a time. The other is the duck. The duck does each card and nothing more. Walk forward is one step, heel to toe. A Turn is a quarter turn. The duck may not go around anything. If the next step would reach a chair leg, stop and say "Clonk." Then swap.
