How it is scored.

Fewest cards wins. Count every card between Start and End: Repeat and End repeat, and every card under another. A Your card is one card. This is where a loop pays off.

Par is the shortest row the pack's author found. If your class finds a shorter one, the par was wrong, not the class.

A row solves a challenge when the duck ends on the goal, has walked through every tunnel the long way, and has not bumped into a wall or the edge of the board. When a challenge names a card to finish with, that card is last. Every Repeat needs its End repeat.

The board.

Every challenge card below draws its own board. Here is how big a square is.

The board rule

One Walk card moves the duck one square. One Turn card is a quarter turn, where it stands. That is the rule on paper and in Studio, and every par is counted by it.

A real duck

Walk and Turn cards on a duck are timed for now: two seconds each, and the duck does not land on board squares. Measured cards are being tested. So for now the board is played by people and in Studio, and a duck beside it is a trial.

Measured cards

Measured cards walk until the duck's own odometry says one 30 cm square and turn until it says a quarter turn. They are being validated, in Pollen's simulator and then on testers' ducks, before they are switched on.

Squares for people

Tape the board on the floor in 30 cm squares, so a student playing the duck can stand in one. Or draw it on squared paper and walk a finger.

Lessons.

Each one fits a class period with paper only.

Debugging

Find the bug in the dark

Hand out the buggy rows for Facing the dark and Storm walk. Groups act each one out, find the one wrong card, and fix the row with a single swap. In Storm walk the wrong card sits inside a loop.

Ask: why does one wrong card inside a loop go wrong more than once?

Safe endings

End it safely

Stop before the rain ends with Stop. On the board Stop moves nothing, but it is a card and a step, and it counts. Talk about why a machine that has finished should end standing still, in a place people know.

Ask: why does it help everyone to know where the duck is when the row ends?

Decisions

Who decides about the storm?

A row of cards cannot see the sky. In chapter 1 of The Duck Who Stayed, the manual says: Pip does not look. Pip just goes. On a farm a person watches the weather and decides when to bring people, animals and machines in. List what a person can see and hear before a storm.

Ask: what can a person notice about a storm that a row of cards cannot?

Events

When it falls

A When it falls row is what the duck does once it is back on its feet. The board does not play it, but a duck running your row would. Write one with Alarm and Stop for the night field, and test it by having the student playing the duck sit down when you say fall.

Ask: after a fall in the dark, should the duck carry on, or stop and call?

Evidence

We can know that

Run a row and keep a log like Wren's: Look, what the duck did; Tried, what you changed; Heard, the sounds it made. The duck makes sounds; what a sound means is a person's guess. Write only what someone else could check, and keep guesses on their own line.

Ask: which lines in your log could another group check?

Nested loops

The whole night field

The whole night field takes 17 cards and 32 steps, with a Repeat inside a Repeat. Print 4 Repeat and 4 End repeat cards for each group. Unroll the row on paper, every step in order, and count them. Then try to find a row shorter than 17 cards.

Ask: how many steps does the duck take for each card you laid?
