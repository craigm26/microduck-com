How it is scored.

Fewest steps wins. Every card the duck does is one step: each Walk card, each Turn card, and a card like Hello at the end.

Par is the fewest steps any row can take. It is worked out by trying every path on the board, so nobody can beat it.

A row solves a challenge when the duck ends on the goal, has walked through every tunnel the long way, and has not bumped into a wall or the edge of the board. When a challenge names a card to finish with, that card is last.

The board.

Every challenge card below draws its own board. Here is how big a square is.

The board rule

One Walk card moves the duck one square. One Turn card is a quarter turn, where it stands. That is the rule on paper and in Studio, and every par is counted by it.

A real duck

Walk and Turn cards on a duck are timed for now: two seconds each, and the duck does not land on board squares. Measured cards are being tested. So for now the board is played by people and in Studio, and a duck beside it is a trial.

Measured cards

Measured cards walk until the duck's own odometry says one 30 cm square and turn until it says a quarter turn. They are being validated, in Pollen's simulator and then on testers' ducks, before they are switched on.

Squares for people

Tape the board on the floor in 30 cm squares, so a student playing the duck can stand in one. Or draw it on squared paper and walk a finger.

Lessons.

Each one fits a class period with paper only.

Sequences

Plan on paper first

Draw the maze on squared paper. Draw the duck's path as one pencil line, then write a letter for each card along it: F, B, L, R. Only then lay out the cards and act the row out.

Ask: where did the pencil line and the cards disagree?

Walk back

A duck that backs up

Play Back into the nest twice: once turning the duck round, once with Walk back. Count the steps both times. Then try Back out of the boathouse, where there is no room to turn.

Ask: when is walking backward better than turning round?

Gates

Through the tunnel

Make a tunnel from a shoebox with both ends cut off and stand it on its square. The duck has to walk through it the long way. Walking in from the side does not count, and neither does starting inside it.

Ask: from which squares can the duck walk into the tunnel?

Debugging

Bump!

When a row walks into a fence or off the board, the duck stops right there. Groups trade rows, act them out, and point to the card that bumped, without starting again from the beginning.

Ask: how did you find the card that bumped?

Endings

Hello at the end

Some challenges say how to finish: Hello at the pond, or a kick at the ball. That card goes last, after the duck has arrived, and it counts as a step.

Ask: what happens if Hello comes before the duck gets there?
