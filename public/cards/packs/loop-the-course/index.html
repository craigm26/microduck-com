How it is scored.

Fewest cards wins. Count every card between Start and End: Repeat and End repeat, and every card under another. A Your card is one card. This is where a loop pays off.

Par is the shortest row the pack's author found. If your class finds a shorter one, the par was wrong, not the class.

A row solves a challenge when the duck ends on the goal, has walked through every tunnel the long way, and has not bumped into a wall or the edge of the board. When a challenge names a card to finish with, that card is last. Every Repeat needs its End repeat.

The board.

Every challenge card below draws its own board. Here is how big a square is.

The board rule

One Walk card moves the duck one square. One Turn card is a quarter turn, where it stands. That is the rule on paper and in Studio, and every par is counted by it.

A real duck

On a real duck, one card has not been measured yet. The first measurement was in a simulator: a Walk forward card carried the duck about 14 cm, and its turns were not quarter turns yet. So for now the board is played by people and in Studio.

Squares for people

Tape the board on the floor in 30 cm squares, so a student playing the duck can stand in one. Or draw it on squared paper and walk a finger.

Lessons.

Each one fits a class period with paper only.

Loops

Say it shorter

Solve Down the long dock with plain cards, then again with Repeat and End repeat, writing the number in Repeat's box. The score is cards: count every card between Start and End, Repeat and End repeat included.

Ask: when does a Repeat save cards, and when does it cost one?

Patterns

Find what repeats

Before laying cards for Round the pond, trace the path with a finger and say each move aloud. Circle the part you said again and again. That part goes between Repeat and End repeat.

Ask: how did you decide where the loop starts?

Debugging

Find the bug

Hand out the buggy row for Who moved the bread? Groups act it out, find the one wrong card, and fix the row with a single swap. Then do the same for Stones the wrong way, where the wrong card sits inside a loop.

Ask: why does one wrong card inside a loop go wrong more than once?

Nested loops

A loop inside a loop

The big corner and Lap and hello need a Repeat inside a Repeat. Unroll one on paper: write out every card the duck does, in order, and count them. Then count the cards you laid.

Ask: how many steps does the duck take for each card you laid?

Parameters

Slow by the reeds

Speed and Look cards go under a card and change how it moves, not where. On the board they change nothing, and each one costs a card. Add Slow under one walk and Look left under another, and watch the score.

Ask: if a card costs a point and does not move the duck, why would you lay it?
