Duck Cards

Duck Cards: the evidence

Can a Microduck walk one square and turn a quarter turn per card, reliably enough for a classroom? We wrote down what reliable means before running anything, ran it in Pollen Robotics' own simulator, ran it again on a different machine, and publish every row. So far the answer is no, and this page says exactly where and why.

Summary

  • Single cards mostly pass. Under randomized physics and sensing, 95 to 98% of the pre-registered short rows end within 10 cm and 15° of where they should.
  • The pre-registered bar is not met. At Studio's 10 Hz state rate, heading sits on the 95% line (96.9% on one machine, 94.9% on another, 93.8% on a repeat), Walk back misses position (92.8% and 93.1%), and the stressed condition fails both.
  • Long rows fail. Of the packs' rows of 10 cards or more, only 33 to 42% land under randomization and none under stress.
  • No falls. 0 falls in 4,128 rows on a quiet machine. Falls seen on an overloaded machine were the machine.
  • So closed-loop cards stay off on real ducks. In the shipped app a duck runs Walk and Turn as timed cards; the board rule is kept by the child and the paper board.

The question

Duck Cards are printed coding cards: a child lays Walk, Turn and Repeat cards in a row and the duck acts them out on a 30 cm grid. A timed card (walk for two seconds) does not land on a square: walking speed varies with the floor, the policy and the battery. A closed-loop card walks until odometry says one square and turns until the heading says a quarter turn. The question is whether that is good enough, measured, before any child relies on it.

System under test

  • Simulator: Pollen Robotics' duck-sim with Pollen's own robotd answering JSON-RPC robot.move, and Pollen's v8 velocity-standing policy walking the duck. Odometry is robotd's own estimate; errors are judged against MuJoCo's true pose.
  • Envelope: commands are clamped to Pollen's gamepad defaults, vx within 0.4 m/s, vy within 0.3 m/s, vyaw within 1.0 rad/s.
  • Controller: duck-card-loop/1. The row keeps a board: every card targets an absolute pose on it, so a card corrects what the last one left. Walk cards steer onto the line; Turn cards stop short by their measured coast, settle and trim. Dead bands from system identification: forward starts near 0.20 to 0.25 m/s, back near -0.35 m/s, turning near 0.5 to 0.625 rad/s.

Pre-registered criteria

Written into docs/PACKS.md on 2026-10-15, before any result was in, and not changed since. A condition passes only if every criterion passes.

  • Position: at least 95% of rows end within 10 cm of the target.
  • Heading: at least 95% of rows end within 15° of the target.
  • Falls: the upper end of the Wilson 95% interval on the fall rate is at most 1% (with zero falls, that takes 381 rows).
  • Time: no Walk card over 8 s and no Turn card over 6 s.
  • Ending badly: every row that fails says so to the child.

Every share on this page is the observed share with its Wilson 95% interval in brackets.

Evidence ladder

  1. Rung 1: Pollen's duck-sim, Monte Carlo. Every criterion in every condition, for the pre-registered rows and separately for rows with a Walk back card. Not passed.
  2. Rung 2: a second, clean machine. The same commit and conditions from a fresh checkout on different hardware, matching every verdict. Run on 2026-10-16 on Hugging Face Jobs (x86, AMD EPYC); 38 of 40 verdicts matched, so not reproduced.
  3. Rung 3: a real duck on a taped 30 cm grid. Studio's Measure my duck protocol, tape-measured and filmed. Not started; any fall sends the work back to rung 1.

Only when all three pass does a card pack turn closed-loop cards on for real ducks.

A rejected candidate

duck-card-loop/2 gave Walk cards the lead Turn cards have: stop short by the measured coast, settle, and lead later Walk cards by the row's mean coast. Its parameters were chosen on development seeds only, then evaluated once on fresh seeds (4,800 rows), on both machines. Against /1 on the same kind of machine:

  • Walk back position at randomized 10 Hz: FAIL 93.1% to PASS 96.3%.
  • Walk back heading, randomized: PASS 98.3% to FAIL 92.5%.
  • Long rows' position at randomized 10 Hz: 36.7% to 20.0%.
  • Falls: 11 on the overloaded first machine, 0 in 4,800 rows on the second.

It trades one failing criterion for another and makes long rows worse. Not adopted.

Why long rows fail

The controller already aims every card at an absolute place on the board, so its own errors do not stack. What stacks is what the duck believes about itself. A heading sensor drifting at up to 0.5°/s is off by up to 30° after a minute, and a minute is about a ten-card row; odometry that reads 5% long or short is off by 5% of every metre walked. Neither is visible to the controller. The enemy is elapsed time and distance, not the number of cards, and no per-card tuning removes a wrong sensor reading.

What is being tried next

Each is a new controller version, held to the same criteria above, evaluated on fresh seeds on a quiet machine, and published here whether it passes or not. One at a time, in this order:

  1. Calibrate at every stop. Standing still, the true turn rate is zero, so whatever the sensor reports is drift: measure it before the row and at every settle, and subtract it.
  2. The mat as a compass. Grid lines seen by the duck's camera fix heading to the nearest quarter turn, and position to the nearest square.
  3. Spend less time. Merge consecutive Walk cards into one walk and two quarter turns into a half turn, so a row takes less time to drift.
  4. A reset in the game. A nest square where the child re-seats the duck, and long challenges split into chapters no longer than the length that passes.

Separately, Measure my duck will record how much a real duck's heading drifts standing still. If that argues for a gentler simulated condition, the condition is added and pre-registered as new; the conditions above are not edited.

Reproduce it

From a fresh checkout of duck-studio on Linux (x86 or arm64), with nothing else running:

sh cardreader/sim/validate.sh
python3 cardreader/sim/mc/mc_summary.py cardreader/sim/results/raw \
  cardreader/sim/results/raw-acceptance cardreader/sim/results/raw-back

validate.sh builds Pollen's stack at pinned commits, runs system identification and every Monte Carlo batch, and writes HOST.txt (machine, commit, Pollen SHAs) and a per-minute load log. Each session's randomness is seeded from the batch seed and the rows already done, so a rerun is a fresh statistical sample, not a replay: compare verdicts and intervals, not rows. A loaded machine makes simulated bodies fall behind real time and fall; check the logs for "behind real time" before trusting a fall.

Limits

  • No real duck has run a closed-loop card yet.
  • duck-sim drives position servos, which are not the actuator v8 was trained with.
  • Real floors, real IMU drift and real foot slip appear only through the randomized ranges. The simulated drift is a constant rate per row; a real one wanders.
  • The second machine is shared hardware; its load reading covers the host, not the job. No simulated body logged falling behind real time.
